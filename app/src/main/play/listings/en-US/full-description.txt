Goosescope tells you the color of whatever your camera points at. It was made for colorblind people, so a red apple, a green leaf or a brown sweater is never a guess again.

POINT AND READ
Aim the circle in the middle of the screen at an object. One of 11 simple color names appears in big letters, with the closest shade: "Red — Scarlet", "Blue — Navy", "Orange — Peach". The name stays steady even if your hand moves a little.

FREEZE TO LEARN MORE
Freeze the image to see the exact shade among 122, with:
• a description of what the color feels like, for someone who may never have seen it ("warm, like late-afternoon sun through a curtain");
• how the color you aimed at compares ("this shade has slightly more red and feels warmer");
• things in nature that share this shade.
Tap anywhere on the frozen image to check another spot, or hide the sheet to see the whole picture.

QUICK TO REACH
Add the Goosescope tile to your quick settings: one tap opens the camera, even on the lock screen, without unlocking your phone.

PRIVATE BY DESIGN
• Works fully offline: the app does not have the internet permission.
• No account, no ads, no tracking.
• Images are never saved or sent. The camera is the only permission.

Also tells you when it is too dark to name a color, with a light button. English and French, switchable in the app.

Goosescope is free and open source (MIT license).
